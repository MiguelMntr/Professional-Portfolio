TOPOLOGY OPTIMIZED STEERING SUPPORT
PRESS FOR DETAILS
Abaqus nTop Inventor Metals

Design of a topology optimized SLM steel support using nTop and Abaqus

× TOPOLOGY OPTIMIZED STEERING SUPPORT

Objectives & Requirements

For Formula Student FEUP's second racecar prototype, a steering support had to be designed to integrate the steering column's gear housing and the autonomous system's actuator. The design had the following requirements:

  • Maximum displacement ≤ 0.010mm;
  • Maximum mass ≤ 1.30kg, a 30% reduction from the machined solution.

Methodology

A design volume was first defined using the prototype's global assembly, considering the column's mounting sequence, the tools' accessibility and the SLM printer bed size - which forced the support to be divided in parts.

The volume was imported into nTop and optimized for compliance considering two load cases - one applying the torque exerted by the autonomous system and the other using the driver loads imposed on the steering wheel.

After the optimization, preliminary FEA analyses were performed in nTop. If the version passed requirements, the geometry was refined in CAD and a final simulation was performed in Abaqus with a detailed model.

Results

The final version achieved:

  • A mass of 0.83kg, representing a 55% reduction;
  • A maximum displacement of 0.008mm.

INFLUENCE OF INSERTS ON FLEXURAL STIFFNESS
PRESS FOR DETAILS
Abaqus Inventor Matlab Composites Metals

Experimental and numerical analysis of the influence of inserts on the flexural stiffness of composite sandwich panels

× INFLUENCE OF INSERTS ON FLEXURAL STIFFNESS

Objectives & Requirements

This study was performed in collaboration with Formula Student FEUP and Adamastor Hypercars for my bachelor's thesis. The requirements for the project were the following:

  • Determine the impact of secondary TTC inserts on the flexural stiffness of sandwich panels;
  • Create an analytical tool to size TTC inserts;
  • Define a numerical model for the sandwich panel's elastic behaviour, achieving a relative error <20 %.

Methodology

To first define the specimens construction, a quasi-isotropic layup was selected and a secondary insert was sized - using a self-developed analytical tool based on the ECSS insert handbook.

In order to properly emulate the manufacturing of a monocoque, a three-step cure cycle was defined to produce the specimens. These were then subjected to a four-point bending test in accordance with ASTM D7250/D7250M-06.

The numerical model used S8R elements to represent the specimens, while the supports and load applicator were defined as rigid elements. Normal contact conditions were established to accurately portrain the physical tests.

Results

Both the sizing tool and numerical models were successfully created. When comparing the simulation results, it was concluded that:

  • The panel stiffness had a relative error of 16-17%;
  • A considerable discrepancy in failure loads was caused by the shell elements' incapability of processing shear loads.

STATIC ANALYSIS OF A WING BOX
PRESS FOR DETAILS
Abaqus Python Matlab Metals

Finite element analysis of a wing box and safety margin computation

× STATIC ANALYSIS OF A WING BOX

Objectives & Requirements

This project is the combined work of two modules from the MSc. in Aeronautical and Vehicular Structures at FEUP - one of these projects was developed in collaboration with a colleague. The requirements were:

  • To develop an FEA model to analyse a wing box under aerodynamic and inertial loads;
  • Analyse different failure mechanisms and compute the respective safety margins.

Methodology

To first determine the loads acting on the aircraft, both the flight envelope and the inertial loads were defined using MatLab.

Using Abaqus, the wing box was then modelled and the loads applied in the respective centres of gravity and pressure. Using a self-developed post-processing script in python, the stress results and internal forces and moments were extracted.

By inputing the material and geometrical properties into excel sheets, a python code was developed allowing to quickly compute the needed tension, compression and semi-diagonal tension safety margins.

Results

All the models and scripts were successfully created and implemented, thus achieving a final grades of 19/20 and 15/20.

BRAKE PEDAL FAILURE ANALYSIS
PRESS FOR DETAILS
Abaqus MatLab Metals

Finite element analysis of a Formula Student brake pedal assembly after reported structural issues

× BRAKE PEDAL FAILURE ANALYSIS

Objectives & Requirements

This work was performed for Formula Student FEUP and an MSc. in Aeronautical and Vehicular Structures module at FEUP. This analysis comes as a consequence of a scrutineering complaint regarding the brake pedal's fixed base compliance, therefore this report aimed to assess:

  • Assess whether the structural integrity was compromised, meaning the safety factor was below 1.1;
  • Perform linear elastic, nonlinear elastoplastic and a nonlinear analysis with contact.

Methodology

For an initial approach, a simplified analytical analysis was conducted to determine the load transfer between components and the maximum displacement of the fixture.

Considering the load cases calculated in the analytical approach, these were then imposed on the fixed base and both a linear elastic and nonlinear elastoplastic analysis were performed. The full assembly model was then created, portraying not only the contact conditions between bases, but also the bolt connections through multi-point constraints.

Results

The analysis showed that:

  • The fixed base would not have failed, however the assembly analysis showed that the adjustable base would have yielded but not ruptured under critical loading - thus the safety factor <1.0;
  • The maximum displacement of the fixed base neared 1.2mm, validated by the full model and the analytical approach.

FORMULA STUDENT PARAMETRIC MONOCOQUE DESIGN
PRESS FOR DETAILS
Siemens NX Notion Composites Metals

Created a parametric model to ease the iteration of new monocoque geometries

× FORMULA STUDENT PARAMETRIC MONOCOQUE DESIGN

Objectives & Requirements

Formula Student FEUP heavily invested in research and development to make the transition to a monocoque chassis. I was responsible for the creation of a highly adaptable parametric CAD model. This task had the following requirements:

  • The model must adapt itself to new suspension geometries and no-go zones;
  • The surface design must facilitate manufacturing by ensuring a minimum fibre bend radius, mounting point edge clearance and flat surfaces priority;
  • Achieve an estimated weight of ≤ 30kg;

Methodology

Firstly, the critical components and the driver templates were converted into no-go zones and their layout was defined based on the weight distribution and CoG height.

A master sketch framework driven by surface-surface intersections was adopted to create the tub's wireframe, ensuring a predictable surface behaviour. Multi-section lofts and boundary patches were then used to create the nose, cockpit and rear surfaces.

Since each section is designed independently to prevent cyclic errors, and are then linked to form the main component. Surface and edge blends were then applied based on the minimum radius of the inner shell and the core's maximum bending.

Finally, parametric tests and surface analysis were performed to evaluate model flexibility and surface continuity.

Results

The preliminary design presented an estimated mass of 24kg. All other requirements were implemented throughout the model.

MOULD FOR CARBON FIBRE SEAT
PRESS FOR DETAILS
Inventor Composites

Design for manufacturing of a mould for carbon fibre seat

× MOULD FOR CARBON FIBRE SEAT

Objectives & Requirements

As a member of Formula Student FEUP, I was designed a mould for the carbon fibre seat with the following requirements:

  • Ensure ease of manufacturing and stock efficiency;
  • Maintain a minimum 2° draft angle across all vertical surfaces;
  • Achieve a suitable surface for a vacuum-assisted hand lay-up process.

Methodology

To develop the mould, the lay-up surface was first selected and the seat design was then adapted to prevent mechanical lock when releasing the part.

For the mould modelling, an offset and trim approach was used to ensure a streamlined machining process. The final design was then analysed and divided based on the foam stock available, ensuring minimal waste.

The stock mould blocks were positioned and then glued before being machined by a 3-axis CNC. Post-machining the mould surface was sealed, micro-filled and sanded up to 800 grit. The seat was then produced using hand layup.

Results

The finalized mould allowed the composite seat to be produced without any demoulding damage or structural flaws.

Responsible for leading the primary structure, packaging and driver interface design and manufacturing

× HEAD OF CHASSIS DEPARTMENT

Objectives & Requirements

As Head of the chassis department at Formula Student FEUP, I had the following requirements:

  • Manage 3 subsystems, those being primary structure (PS), packaging (PA) and driver interface (DI);
  • Oversee and coordinate 8 team members;
  • Conclude the manufacturing of the second prototype, lead the design of the third spaceframe and initiate R&D of the team's first monocoque.

Methodology

I became department lead mid spaceframe production, facing delays in the critical-path due to a lack of budget. Together with the board, we altered the sponsor manufacturing schedules and simplified components to reduce the number of processes needed. Afterwards, I coordinated the integration of the remaining subsystems into the prototype.

At the start of the new season, I centralized the department's operations on Notion and set clear requirements for each subsystem. To increase efficiency through the design phase, I drove the automation of repetitive CAD and analysis tasks through custom scripts, templates, and applications.

To ground new members in design for manufacturing, they were tasked with generating cost quotes for their designs and to participate in specimen production. This minimized downstream manufacturing bottlenecks and accelerated the production schedule.

Results

This experience strengthened my project coordination under tight resource constraints and enabled the team to streamline design workflows, deliver the spaceframe design on schedule and establish a solid R&D foundation for future development.
